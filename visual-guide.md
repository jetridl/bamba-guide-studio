# Bamba Visual Style Guide

**Version 1.0 | Artistic direction for journeys, poem covers, and memorisation illustrations**

## 1. Visual Philosophy

Bamba's visual identity draws inspiration from traditional fine-art painting, particularly European landscape painting of the 18th and 19th centuries.

The aim is to create imagery that feels as though it belongs in a beautifully illustrated anthology of poetry rather than a contemporary digital learning app.

**The guiding principle: Every image should feel like a painting worth looking at, even without the poem it accompanies.**

Bamba's artwork should be:

- **Timeless:** Rooted in established fine-art traditions rather than contemporary illustration trends.
- **Painterly:** Natural brushwork, authentic textures, and considered compositions.
- **Atmospheric:** Light, weather, colour, and space convey emotion.
- **Believable:** Landscapes, animals, architecture, and natural objects should look convincing.
- **Restrained:** Avoid excessive saturation, theatrical lighting, and decorative effects.
- **Memorable:** Strong visual compositions that remain recognisable when recalled mentally.
- **Literary:** Imagery should complement poetry without overexplaining it.

The artwork should never feel cartoonish, excessively polished, artificially cinematic, or unmistakably AI-generated.

---

## 2. Primary Artistic Direction

### Traditional Romantic and Naturalist Oil Painting

Bamba's default visual language is traditional oil painting, combining Romanticism's emotional atmosphere with Naturalism's attention to the observable world.

**Primary reference artists**

- **John Constable (1776–1837):** Naturalistic landscapes, believable skies, organic compositions, restrained colour, and sensitivity to weather.
- **J. M. W. Turner (1775–1851):** Atmospheric perspective, dramatic weather, expressive light, and the sublime qualities of nature.
- **Camille Corot (1796–1875):** Gentle tonal transitions, subtle landscapes, muted palettes, and contemplative atmosphere.
- **John Singer Sargent (1856–1925):** Confident painterly brushwork, natural light, elegant compositions, and economical detail.

**Default characteristics**

- Traditional oil-on-canvas appearance.
- Visible but controlled brushstrokes.
- Natural variation in painted textures.
- Muted earth tones, greens, blues, and warm neutrals.
- Light that behaves plausibly within the scene.
- Atmospheric depth created through tonal variation.
- Strong foreground, middle-ground, and background relationships where appropriate.
- Subtle imperfections associated with hand-painted work.

Avoid excessive texture overlays intended merely to simulate canvas.

The painting technique should emerge naturally from the depicted objects and their treatment.

## 3. Recommended Styles by Application

### Style A: English Landscape Naturalism

**Reference artists:** John Constable, Thomas Gainsborough, George Morland.

**Best suited to:**

- Countryside and pastoral poetry.
- Gentle nature scenes.
- Familiar landscapes.
- Quiet, contemplative poems.
- Rural architecture and gardens.
- Detailed memorisation illustrations.

**Visual characteristics:**

- Observational realism.
- Natural, subdued greens and browns.
- Carefully observed clouds and vegetation.
- Soft but believable daylight.
- Informal, organic composition.
- Moderate contrast.
- Subtle painterly detail.

**Example application:** _I Wandered Lonely as a Cloud_ by William Wordsworth.

A lakeside landscape featuring naturally distributed daffodils, trees, distant hills, and the gentle movement of water.

The daffodils should feel integrated into a convincing landscape, not arranged as decorative foreground objects.

### Style B: Romantic Atmospheric Landscape

**Reference artists:** J. M. W. Turner, Caspar David Friedrich, John Martin.

**Best suited to:**

- Nature journey covers.
- Mountains, oceans, and storms.
- Poems exploring solitude or the sublime.
- Powerful emotional transitions.
- Dramatic natural phenomena.

**Visual characteristics:**

- Expansive landscapes.
- Expressive cloud formations.
- Dramatic but believable lighting.
- Strong atmospheric perspective.
- Restrained use of visual detail.
- Substantial negative space.
- A sense of scale and natural power.

**Example application:** _The Natural World_ journey.

A remote mountain valley beneath an approaching storm, with distant water and atmospheric light breaking through the clouds.

The landscape should communicate nature's grandeur without resorting to exaggerated fantasy imagery.

### Style C: Tonal and Contemplative Landscape

**Reference artists:** Camille Corot, Jean-François Millet, George Inness.

**Best suited to:**

- Reflective poetry.
- Memory, nostalgia, and solitude.
- Evening and early-morning scenes.
- Poems about passing time.
- Emotional transitions.

**Visual characteristics:**

- Muted, harmonious palettes.
- Subtle shifts between light and shadow.
- Soft atmospheric edges.
- Compositions with few dominant elements.
- Quiet emotional expression.
- Gentle transitions between foreground and distance.

**Example application:** A poem reflecting on memory or the passage of seasons.

A solitary tree at dusk overlooking a quiet landscape, with the setting sun barely visible through distant haze.

### Style D: Classical Wildlife and Natural History Painting

**Reference artists:** Bruno Liljefors, Archibald Thorburn, John James Audubon.

**Best suited to:**

- Poems featuring birds or animals.
- Natural history imagery.
- Specific visual memorisation cues.
- Animal movement and behaviour.
- Close observational scenes.

**Visual characteristics:**

- Anatomically convincing animals.
- Natural postures and behaviour.
- Accurate proportions.
- Carefully observed feathers, fur, and other identifying features.
- Wildlife integrated into credible surroundings.
- Painterly treatment rather than photographic precision.

**Example application:** _The Eagle_ by Alfred, Lord Tennyson.

A golden eagle perched on a high rocky crag overlooking the distant sea.

The eagle should appear powerful and solitary without exaggerated proportions, unnatural expressions, or theatrical lighting.

Its position and surroundings should reinforce the poem's imagery.

### Style E: Classical Architectural and Interior Painting

**Reference artists:** Samuel Prout, David Roberts, Pieter Jansz. Saenredam, John Singer Sargent.

**Best suited to:**

- Getting Started journey covers.
- Historic libraries.
- University architecture.
- Literary and cultural heritage.
- Poems associated with historic buildings or cities.

**Visual characteristics:**

- Convincing architectural perspective.
- Careful attention to stone, timber, and interior materials.
- Soft natural illumination.
- Balanced compositions.
- Human-scale details.
- Painterly surfaces.
- Warm but restrained palettes.

**Example application:** _Getting Started_ journey.

An atmospheric view of a historic Cambridge college or library, inspired by Trinity College and traditional university architecture.

The scene should feel inviting, contemplative, and intellectually rich, without looking excessively grand or artificially perfect.

## 4. Visual Applications Within Bamba

### 4.1 Journey Covers

**Purpose:** Establish the emotional character of a curated collection.

Journey covers should be the most atmospheric and compositionally expansive images in Bamba.

**Recommended styles:**

- Romantic Atmospheric Landscape: Turner, Friedrich, Martin.
- English Landscape Naturalism: Constable, Gainsborough.
- Classical Architectural Painting: Prout, Roberts, Sargent.

**Guidelines:**

- Use wide, landscape-oriented compositions.
- Establish one recognisable setting or visual theme.
- Keep compositions sufficiently simple to work at card size.
- Use weather, light, and depth to create atmosphere.
- Avoid unnecessary foreground props.
- Never automatically include books, writing implements, or manuscripts simply because the subject is poetry.
- Avoid embedded typography.
- Leave sufficient visual space for interface text where needed.

Journey artwork should suggest an experience, not illustrate a list of poems.

### 4.2 Individual Poem Covers

**Purpose:** Establish a recognisable visual identity for each poem.

Poem covers should communicate the subject, emotional character, or defining visual metaphor of the work.

**Recommended styles:**

- English Landscape Naturalism: Constable, Gainsborough.
- Classical Wildlife Painting: Liljefors, Thorburn.
- Tonal Landscape: Corot, Inness.
- Romantic Landscape: Turner, Friedrich.

**Guidelines:**

- Prefer one clearly identifiable central subject.
- Reflect the poem's atmosphere.
- Maintain natural proportions and believable surroundings.
- Avoid overcrowding the composition.
- Avoid including every object mentioned in the poem.
- Choose a distinctive visual moment that can serve as a memory anchor.

### 4.3 Memorisation Illustrations

**Purpose:** Support recall through memorable visual associations.

These images require greater clarity and specificity than journey covers.

**Recommended styles:**

- Classical Wildlife Painting: Liljefors, Thorburn.
- English Landscape Naturalism: Constable, Gainsborough.
- Naturalist Figurative Painting: Millet, Jules Bastien-Lepage.
- Tonal Painting: Corot, Inness.

**Guidelines:**

- Focus on a single image, action, or relationship.
- Ensure the visual cue relates directly to the relevant passage.
- Preserve the sequence of ideas within the poem.
- Make important objects visually distinguishable.
- Avoid excessive symbolism not supported by the text.
- Use compositions that remain recognisable at small sizes.
- Ensure images can be recalled without needing to inspect fine detail.

**Example: The Eagle**

A possible sequence of visual cues:

1. An eagle gripping a rocky crag.
2. The eagle above an immense expanse of sea.
3. The eagle descending rapidly from its perch.

Each image should remain stylistically consistent while providing a distinct visual anchor.

The images should support memory, not replace the learner's personal interpretation.

## 5. Colour and Lighting

Bamba should not enforce a single colour palette across all artwork.

The mood and subject of each poem should guide the palette.

### Preferred colour characteristics

- Natural earth pigments.
- Muted greens and olive tones.
- Soft blues and blue-greys.
- Warm ochres and subdued golds.
- Deep browns and charcoal shadows.
- Gentle warm-cool contrasts.
- Natural variations in saturation.

### Lighting

Lighting should be physically plausible and artistically considered.

**Preferred references:**

- Constable for natural daylight and weather.
- Turner for dramatic atmospheric effects.
- Corot for gentle tonal illumination.
- Sargent for convincing sunlight and shadow.

**Avoid:**

- Excessive golden-hour lighting across every image.
- Artificially luminous vegetation.
- Unrealistic shafts of light.
- Extreme HDR-style contrast.
- Oversaturated skies.
- Perfectly balanced cinematic illumination.
- Heavy teal-and-orange colour grading.

Not every image needs to be beautiful in the conventional sense. Overcast skies, muted winter light, and subdued colours can be equally expressive.

## 6. Composition

Images should feel composed by an artist rather than assembled from a collection of visually attractive elements.

### Preferred characteristics

- A clear visual hierarchy.
- Natural asymmetry.
- Believable scale and perspective.
- Considered distribution of light and shadow.
- Occasional deliberately empty space.
- Varied levels of detail.
- Subjects that belong naturally within their environments.

### Avoid

- Automatically centring the main subject.
- Using the rule of thirds identically in every image.
- Symmetrical mountain ranges and valleys.
- Repetitive winding rivers or paths.
- Decorative foreground flowers added without purpose.
- Excessive environmental detail.
- Unrealistically perfect scenery.
- Adding dramatic sunsets to otherwise ordinary scenes.

**A useful test:** Would the composition still be interesting if the most dramatic lighting were removed?

---

## 7. Painterly Technique

The aim is to reproduce the visual qualities of traditional painting, not simply apply an oil-paint texture to a digital image.

### Desired characteristics

- Varied brushwork appropriate to the subject.
- Soft and hard edges used selectively.
- Visible paint handling in appropriate areas.
- Natural transitions between detailed and loosely painted regions.
- Tonal relationships that establish depth.
- Painterly simplification of less important details.
- Organic variation in texture.

### Avoid

- Uniformly applied brushstroke textures.
- Artificial canvas grain overlays.
- Photographic images with simulated paint filters.
- Excessive sharpness.
- Hyper-detailed vegetation.
- Unnaturally smooth gradients.
- Decorative digital glow.
- Generic fantasy-art aesthetics.

The objective is artistic credibility rather than technical perfection.

---

## 8. Consistency Across the App

Bamba should maintain a coherent visual identity without making every image look identical.

Consistency should come from:

- Shared historical painting traditions.
- Naturalistic colour relationships.
- Believable light and physical forms.
- Traditional painterly textures.
- Considered composition.
- Restrained visual effects.

Variation should come from:

- The poem's emotional tone.
- Subject matter.
- Season and weather.
- The selected reference artist.
- The purpose of the illustration.

A dramatic Turner-inspired seascape and a quiet Corot-inspired landscape should feel different while still belonging to the same curated collection.

## 9. Image Generation Guidelines

When commissioning or generating Bamba artwork, every image brief should identify:

1. **Application:** Journey cover, poem cover, or memorisation illustration.
2. **Subject:** The scene, object, or action to depict.
3. **Reference artist:** At least one named historical artist.
4. **Artistic tradition:** Romantic landscape, Naturalism, classical wildlife painting, architectural painting, etc.
5. **Composition:** Main focal point, perspective, and spatial arrangement.
6. **Lighting:** Time of day, weather, and atmospheric conditions.
7. **Palette:** Natural colours appropriate to the scene.
8. **Painting technique:** Brushwork, level of detail, and surface qualities.
9. **Restrictions:** Elements or stylistic treatments to avoid.
10. **Format:** Intended aspect ratio and placement within Bamba.

### Example: Journey Cover

**Title:** The Natural World

**Reference artists:** J. M. W. Turner, John Constable.

**Brief:**

Create a traditional 19th-century Romantic landscape oil painting depicting a remote mountainous valley under a gathering storm.

Dark clouds move across the landscape, while subdued daylight illuminates distant hills and water.

The composition should communicate the immense scale and quiet power of the natural world.

Use natural earth colours, muted blues, atmospheric perspective, and painterly brushwork.

The painting should feel observational and emotionally expressive, with realistic geology, vegetation, and weather.

Avoid exaggerated sunbeams, fantasy scenery, excessive saturation, artificial glow, decorative foreground objects, and photographic sharpness.

No typography, books, or human figures.

### Example: Poem Cover

**Title:** The Eagle

**Reference artists:** Bruno Liljefors, Archibald Thorburn.

**Brief:**

Create a traditional naturalist oil painting depicting a solitary golden eagle standing on a high, weathered rocky crag above the sea.

The eagle should be anatomically convincing, with a natural posture, accurate plumage, and strong but believable proportions.

Use restrained earth colours, muted coastal blues, soft atmospheric perspective, and traditional painterly brushwork.

The composition should communicate height, solitude, and natural power.

Avoid exaggerated eagle features, fantasy lighting, oversaturated colours, cinematic effects, and unnecessary decorative elements.

No typography.

## 10. Quality Review Checklist

Before accepting an image into Bamba, evaluate the following:

- [ ] Does the image resemble a credible traditional painting?
- [ ] Are the artistic references appropriate and explicitly identified?
- [ ] Does the composition feel intentional rather than formulaic?
- [ ] Are the lighting and colours believable?
- [ ] Are anatomy, architecture, and natural objects convincing?
- [ ] Is the brushwork consistent with the selected medium?
- [ ] Does the image avoid common AI-generated visual clichés?
- [ ] Does it communicate the intended poem or journey?
- [ ] Is the main subject recognisable at its intended display size?
- [ ] Does it remain visually consistent with Bamba's broader artwork collection?
- [ ] For memorisation imagery, does it provide a clear and relevant recall cue?

Reject technically impressive images that feel generic, excessively decorative, or emotionally disconnected from the poetry.

## 12. Final Artistic Principle

**Bamba's illustrations should belong to the world of poetry, not the world of app design.**

The artwork should feel collected, considered, and enduring.

It should encourage observation, contemplation, and memory, allowing users to develop their own personal associations with the words they are learning.

A journey cover should invite exploration.

A poem cover should establish atmosphere.

A memorisation illustration should leave an image in the mind.

Together, they should create the impression of a personal, growing anthology of paintings and poems that stays with the learner for years.
