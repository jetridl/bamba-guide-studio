REFERENCE STRUCTURE

The established catalogue guides were inspected, including Who Has Seen the Wind?,
The Eagle, Hope is the thing with feathers, and The Road Not Taken. Their opening
recaps return to two days ago with Random Words and to yesterday with First and
Last Word, before the whole-poem and current-day reading. Their practice sections
make room for commentary and other text between exercise groups.

The five newer guides now follow that structure across all 21 of their learning
days, while retaining the five-round order explicitly requested by the editor.

OPENING EACH DAY

Day 1 has no previous-day recap.
Day 2 opens with A Look Back, the existing reading of yesterday's passage, and
First and Last Word exercises for every chunk of yesterday's new lines.
Day 3 onward opens with A Look Back, Random Words exercises for every chunk from
two days ago, the existing reading of yesterday's passage, and First and Last Word
exercises for every chunk from yesterday.

Recaps cover only those one or two immediately preceding learning days. They do
not use today's new lines or extend to all earlier days. Each recap exercise has
an explicit chunk reference and recapOffsetDays metadata. All chunks are the
existing one- or two-line chunks; no poem structure was split or replaced.

AFTER THE RECAP

The whole poem is available to read, followed by the current day's passage and
the original commentary. The five practice groups are interspersed as follows:

Last Word
  New, passage-specific note on sound or form
First Word
  Existing Let the words return reading/recall page
First and Last Word
  New, passage-specific note on imagery or meaning
Random Words
  New, passage-specific note on reading aloud
First Letter
  Existing reflection, final whole-poem return where present, and closing

Each group still covers every current-day chunk once, in its original order.
Parts within the same exercise group stay together, with a meaningful reading or
text page between each pair of different exercise groups. The added notes are
specific to the current passage: they discuss its sounds, images, sentence turns
and voice rather than adding generic pause screens.
