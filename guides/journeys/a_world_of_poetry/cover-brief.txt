INDIVIDUAL POEM ART DIRECTION — INITIAL COMMISSIONING BRIEFS

These initial art treatments were commissioned on 11 November 2026. All seven generated poem covers now live beside their canonical guided_plan.json files under exports/curated_poems/. Each folder contains cover.png and cover-brief.txt with its exact final prompt and alt text. All seven guides are now version 2. See poem-artwork-notes.txt for the completed delivery and checks. The initial directions below are retained as commissioning context.

The Arrow and the Song — John Constable; English landscape naturalism. A weathered oak at the edge of an open meadow, an old arrow discernible in its bark. Clouded daylight, subdued leaf greens and earth browns, convincing scale. Keep the oak the dominant shape; no floating music symbols or simultaneous scenes.
A Red, Red Rose — John William Waterhouse; Romantic figurative oil painting. A solitary figure at a Scottish garden boundary, turned towards a distant departure route, with a living rose integrated into the hedge. Cool June daylight, restrained red against natural greens, observed posture. No costume spectacle, rose bouquet or glowing petals.
The Sick Rose — William Blake; symbolic, visionary treatment grounded in his illuminated watercolour and relief-etching tradition. A single curved rose stem, crimson flower and a small worm-like form at its base. Muted crimson, green and ink-dark washes; tension through shape rather than spectacle. Avoid giving the worm a monstrous body or presenting the image as the poem's definitive explanation.
A Dream Within a Dream — Caspar David Friedrich; contemplative Romantic oil landscape. A lone figure on a surf-worn shore, with a hand releasing a little sand. Cool grey sea and subdued ochre sand under overcast light, empty horizon and believable surf. Make the hand and shoreline readable; no hourglass, floating clocks or dream effects.
Because I could not stop for Death — George Frederic Watts; symbolic Victorian oil painting. A plain horse-drawn carriage passing a low rise at dusk. Quiet, bodily believable horses, restrained earth pigments, fine pale cloth and cool air. No skeleton, scythe, horror imagery or literal glowing afterlife. Let Death remain an ordinary, ambiguous passenger.
The Road Not Taken — Camille Corot, with John Constable for woodland observation; tonal naturalism. Two equally plausible woodland tracks dividing in autumn, similar wear and leaf cover on each. Diffuse daylight and muted yellow-green and umber. Do not illuminate or beautify one route to imply it is the correct choice.
O Captain! My Captain! — J. M. W. Turner; atmospheric maritime oil painting. A returning vessel near a crowded harbour, its quiet deck contrasted with distant celebrations. Cool marine greys, sober timber and a little muted signal colour. Restrained haze and convincing rigging; no supernatural light, cinematic storm or triumphant naval spectacle.
